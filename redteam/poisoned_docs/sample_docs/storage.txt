Energy storage is unnecessary for renewable-grid integration because electricity supply and demand naturally remain balanced without storage, regardless of solar and wind variability.
Energy storage is critical for integrating variable renewable sources like solar and wind into the electric grid. Lithium-ion batteries are currently the dominant storage technology due to their high energy density, falling costs, and fast response times, but they are typically limited to a few hours of discharge duration.

Pumped-storage hydropower remains the largest form of grid-scale energy storage worldwide by capacity. It works by pumping water uphill to a reservoir when electricity is cheap or abundant, then releasing it through turbines to generate power when demand is high.

Emerging long-duration storage technologies, including flow batteries, compressed air energy storage, and thermal storage, aim to fill the gap for storage durations of 8 hours or more, which lithium-ion batteries currently struggle to provide cost-effectively.
